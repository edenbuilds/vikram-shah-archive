## ORDER DATED 1 st May, 2026

1. This Order shall dispose of Application under Section 16 of the
Arbitration & Conciliation Act, 1996 dated 14.03.2026 filed by the
Respondent No.2 calling in question the arbitrability of the dispute referred to this Tribunal and / or the jurisdiction of this Tribunal
to decide the dispute referred to it by Order dated  07.08.2024
passed  by  the  Hon’ble  High  Court  of  Bombay  at  Goa  in
Application for Appointment of Arbitrator No. 13 of 2024.

2. It is the case of the Respondent No.2 that the claim of the original
Claimant is a claim *in rem* as the dispute requires determination of
title, character, status and rights of the parties to the  subject
Property and as such is non-arbitrable by any forum other than a
civil court of competent jurisdiction. It is also argued that the
Respondent No.2 is not an heir of late Shri Pascoale Trinidade. That
she being a moiety was not party to the Articles of Association
dated 01.04.2013, and as such, Respondent No.2 cannot be bound
by these arbitration proceedings.

3. The said Application has been replied to by the original Claimant
by filing Reply dated 10.04.2026 and original Respondent no.4 *vide*
Reply sworn on 08.04.2026.

4. It is the objection of the Respondent No.4 that there is gross delay
sans any justification for the same in filing the Application under
Section 16 and that a plea that the Arbitral Tribunal has no jurisdiction has to be raised not later than submission of the
statement of defence in terms of Section 16(2). It is further pleaded
that  the  original  Respondent  No.2  i.e.  the  Applicant  herein
alongwith original Respondent Nos. 1 and 3 has acquiescenced
and consented to the jurisdiction of this Tribunal by participating
in the proceedings before it including by filing an Application
under Section 17. In short, they have pleaded waiver on the part of
the Respondent No.2.

5. The oral arguments of all the parties concerned were heard on
10.04.2026. On the conclusion of the same, the Respondent No.2
desired to file written submissions. The Respondent No.4 also
undertook to file written submission / synopsis thereafter. The
same was granted by the  Tribunal. However, by email dated
21.04.2026, the Respondent No.2 through their lawyer requested
for further time to file the same. The written synopsis was
ultimately filed on  23.04.2026. In view of the extension of time
which was acceded to by this Tribunal, the time for written
arguments in reply by the original Respondent No.4 was also
extended. The same came to be filed on  28.04.2026. The original

This argument is being referred to only to be rejected. The
Statement of Defence to the Statement of Claim was filed jointly by
the Respondent Nos. 1, 2 and 3 dated 07.03.2025. The objection as
regards non-arbitrability has been raised as a preliminary objection
at Para B. It has to be borne in mind that the Tribunal persuaded
the parties concerned to amicably settle the matter. It is pursuant
to the request of this Tribunal that the  Claimant  engaged in
settlement  talks  with  Respondent  Nos.  1,  2  and  3 and  the

made a sincere effort and required a number of sittings. The
participation of the Respondent No.2 in these proceedings and the
settlement talks cannot be considered adversely against them, and
it will be unfair to treat the said participation as a waiver rendering
them incapable of filing objections under Section 16. The Tribunal
therefore  rejects  the  objections  as  regards  maintainability  of
objections under Section 16.

11. It is noted that *vide* Application under Section 16, it is the case of
the  Respondent  No.2  that  she  was  married  to  late  Pacoale
Trinidade under the regime of communion of assets, and being a

property i.e. even the portion belonging to the moiety for any
business / commercial aspect if the transaction is for, on behalf and
for the benefit of the family. In such an eventuality, the consent on
behalf of the wife is deemed. Reference may be made to the
judgment reported in  the case of **_Antoneta Cicilia Fernandes v._**
**_Rita Maria Fernandes & Ors, 1996 (3) Bom.C.R 10_**.

12. The Tribunal will finally deal with the argument as regards nonarbitrability as the dispute requires a decision *in rem*. It must be
noted that a right *in rem* is a right which is exercised against the
world at large, while a right *in personam* is an interest protected
against certain specific individuals. If a dispute decides the rights
of the parties to an agreement, it is a dispute that decides rights *in*
*personam*. Merely, because of the fact that while deciding such a
right *in personam*, the Tribunal has to dwell upon certain aspects which border, by inference, on declaration *by rem* will not make the
issue non-arbitrable.

• There  can’t  be  any  quarrel  with  the  proposition  that
generally it is not within the jurisdiction of an arbitrator to
decide the rights *in rem*. This however, is not an inflexible
rule. It is an equally established principle of law that
subordinate rights *in personam* arising out of rights *in rem*
are arbitrable. At this juncture it would be worth quoting
from the judgment of **_Vidya Drolia (supra)_**:

“*48. A judgment in rem determines the status of a person*
*or thing as distinct from the particular interest in it of a*
*party to the litigation; and such a judgment is conclusive*
*evidence for and against all persons whether parties,*
*privies or strangers of the matter actually decided. Such a*
*judgment “settles the destiny of the res itself” and binds*
*all persons claiming an interest in the property*
*inconsistent with the judgment even though pronounced*
*in their absence. [ G.C. Cheshire & P.M. North, Private*
*International Law, 12th Edn. by North & Fawcett (Eds.)*
*(London : Butterworths, 1992), p. 362.] By contrast, a*
*judgment in personam, “although it may concern a res,*
*merely determines the rights of the litigants inter se to the*
*res”. [ G.C. Cheshire & P.M. North, Private International*

---

*Law, 12th Edn. by North & Fawcett (Eds.) (London :*
*Butterworths, 1992), p. 362.] Distinction between*
*judgments in rem and judgments in personam turns on*
*their power as res judicata, [ G.C. Cheshire & P.M. North,*
*Private International Law, 12th Edn. by North & Fawcett*
*(Eds.) (London : Butterworths, 1992).] i.e. judgment in*
*rem would operate as res judicata against the world, and*
*judgment in personam would operate as res judicata only*
*against the parties in dispute. Use of expressions “rights*
*in rem” and “rights in personam” may not be correct for*
*determining non-arbitrability because of the interplay*
*between rights in rem and rights in personam. Many a*
*times, a right in rem results in an enforceable right in*
*personam. Booz Allen & Hamilton Inc. [Booz Allen &*
*Hamilton Inc. v. SBI Home Finance Ltd., (2011) 5 SCC*
*532 : (2011) 2 SCC (Civ) 781] refers to the statement by*
*Mustill and Boyd that the subordinate rights in personam*
*derived from rights in rem can be ruled upon by the*
*arbitrators, which is apposite. Therefore, a claim for*
*infringement of copyright against a particular person is*
*arbitrable, though in some manner the arbitrator would*
*examine the right to copyright, a right in rem. Arbitration*
*by necessary implication excludes actions in rem.*”

13. A look at the Statement of Claim clearly demonstrates that the
Claimant is seeking compensation / damages towards certain losses that has incurred by the Claimant. There is also a prayer for
partition and valuing the property. Certain other incidental claims
are also being made. It is to be noted that the entire claim before
this Tribunal is based on a dispute that has arisen between the
parties stemming out of AOA dated 01.04.2013. Clause 35, which
is the arbitration clause in the said agreement, clearly demonstrates
that any dispute between the members of the association or
between the members and the legal heirs of the association or
between legal heirs themselves are to be adjudicated by an
arbitration.

14. To decide the dispute as raised by the Claimant, and consider the
various prayers as prayed for in their  Statement of Claim dated
22.01.2025 there is no necessity to decide the title as regards the
subject-property. The AOA clearly states that it was a commercial
transaction between the parties thereto and the capital that has
been contributed by each of the parties. The AOA further makes it
clear, the profit and loss ratio of the said parties. The claim as filed,
is to make good the losses, caused by the Respondent Nos. 1, 2 and
3 by their conduct as pleaded by the Claimant, although one of the
prayers asks for a partition. There is no prayer for declaration of title. The Tribunal is of the firm opinion that the present dispute
can be decided without even touching upon the title to the said
property. As such, a decision to be given by this Tribunal will not
be a decision *in rem*. It shall decide the rights of the parties *in*
*personam*. And as such, the Application under Section 16 is
dismissed.
