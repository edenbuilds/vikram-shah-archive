 (I)   Application for amendment of the Statement of Defense filed by

       Respondent Nos. 1, 2 and 3 dated 12.06.2026.


2.     The Application for amendment is filed on the ground that the amendment

       is intended to elaborate, clarify and particularize the existing defenses by

       spelling out the legal effects of the Articles of Association, the nature and

       kind of rights asserted by the Claimant, the status of the Respondent No.2

       as a moiety holder, and effect of Joint Venture Agreement. It is further

       their case that certain documents came to the knowledge and attention of

       Respondent No.2 which necessitated the filing of amendment application,

       and application for production of documents which I shall advert to later

       in this Order.


3.     I heard the arguments on behalf of all the parties. Adv. Shivan Desai on

       behalf of Respondents Nos. 1, 2 and 3 submitted that the amendment is

       necessary and essential to adjudicate the dispute. It is his case that some

       of the proposed averments are more in the nature of a legal ground as

       regards the right of a moiety holder. Certain other averments refer to the

       sale deed executed in 2014 in favour of Mr. Loankar, as also reference is

found to the Joint Venture Agreement and Kuval projects. All the said

     averments are necessary to decide the dispute before this Tribunal. It is

4.   Adv. Bhadbade on behalf of the Claimant has raised serious objections to

     of Defense was filed in 2025 and the application for proposed amendment

     is filed a year later. He contends that Section 23(3) and 23(4) does not

     is further his case that in terms of Section 4 of Bankers Book Evidence

     out can’t be relied upon as the provisions of the said Section has not been

     followed.


5.   Adv. Kotwal appearing on behalf of Respondent No.4 has also objected

     to the amendment on the ground that there are malafides and has taken

     me through the averments in the original Statement of Defense and certain

     documents to show that the averments sought to be incorporated by way

     of amendment is a malafide attempt. He also states that an exercise of this

     nature is futile as he points to the earlier roznamas that in fact Respondent

     Nos. 1, 2 and 3 had agreed to the appointment of a Receiver.

6.   I have considered the arguments and gone through the pleadings and

     documents on record. It is clear from a series of decisions of the Hon’ble

     Supreme Court that a liberal approach has to be adopted to the pre-trial

     amendments. This Tribunal is of further opinion that amendment to the

     written statement / statement of defense cannot be put at the same pedestal

     as an amendment to the plaint / statement of claim. An amendment to the

     written statement / statement of defense deserves more latitude.


7.   The arguments based on malafides are all touching the merits of the claim

     of Respondent Nos. 1, 2 and 3. The said submissions points out to the

     inconsistency of the stands. One of the cardinal principles of law is that

     while deciding application for amendment is that merits of the proposed

     amendment cannot be looked into, and in any case it is not argued that the

     malafides pertain to filing of the application for amendment. It is a fact as

     noted by this Tribunal that the parties were seriously exploring a

     settlement and the matter had to be adjourned on a considerable number

     of occasions for enabling the parties to try and settle the dispute. In these

     circumstances, delay if any in filing of these amendment applications will

     not come in way of the amendments sought to be made.


8.   I have gone through the proposed amendments. They all are necessary

     and essential to adjudicate the present dispute. No prejudice can be caused

9.    The argument based on Section 4 of the Bankers Book Evidence Act has

 (II)   Application for production of Additional Documents filed by

        Respondent No.2 dated 12.06.2026


11.     It’s the case of the Respondent Nos.1, 2 and 3 that the documents now

        sought to be produced came to the attention of the Respondent No.2 only

        recently. The said documents throw light on the case as sought to be

        pleaded by the said Respondents. That the said documents also have

        prompted the Respondent Nos. 1, 2 and 3 to carry out certain amendments

        so as to particularize, clarify and elaborate the Statement of Defence

        including raising of some legal grounds.


12.     It’s argued by Adv. Shivan Desai that these documents are essential to the

        litigation and will further the cause of justice as the same are directly

        related to the Defense which is raised by Respondent Nos. 1, 2 and 3. That

        there was settlement talks between the parties and during this period much

        attention was not paid to the contents of those documents as a sincere

        effort was made towards negotiating a settlement.


13.     Adv. Bhadbade vehemently opposed the Application on the grounds that

        the Application was vague. That the same does not disclose who acquired

        knowledge of the said documents. That the documents are not relevant to

the controversy. He also argued more or less based on the arguments made

      by him as regards the Application for Amendment.


14.   Adv. Kotwal also opposed this application on the grounds that the same

      is malafide. He argued that only certain extracts are sought to be produced

      and not the entire document. He further argued that after having agreed to

      the appointment of a Receiver, the Respondent Nos. 1, 2 and 3 cannot

      now turn around and seek to produce additional documents and proposed

      amendments. He also argued that the said documents do not further the

      cause of Respondent Nos. 1, 2 and 3. He pointed out Section 2A of the

      Bankers Book Evidence Act to show that the said Act applies to

      arbitration proceedings as well.


15.   I have considered the arguments and applied my mind to the pleadings

      and documents on record. The jurisprudence as regards production of

      documents as enunciated by the Hon’ble Supreme Court and the various

      High Courts is that if there are documents which can demonstrate a party’s

      case, they should not be shut out. The concerned parties can cross-

      examine the witness who produces these documents and they have all the

      opportunity to advance the argument that the said documents do not

      advance the cause of the person seeking to produce it.

16.   As regards Bankers Book Evidence Act, no doubt the same applies to

      arbitration proceedings. But at this stage, it becomes irrelevant as the

      Tribunal is not called upon to decide on the admissibility of the same. The

      same shall be considered at the stage of evidence.


17.   In terms of Section 19 of the Arbitration Act, the Arbitral Tribunal is not

      bound by Civil Procedure Code or the Evidence Act, therefore the

      technicalities concerning documents does not apply to arbitral tribunal. I

      have seen the documents which are sought to be produced. It can’t be said

      that the same are not relevant to the dispute in question.


18.   At this stage, the Tribunal is not considering whether the said documents

      prove or disprove the case of Respondent Nos. 1, 2 and 3.


19.   It is true that the parties did consider settlement and seriously worked

      towards the same. It is also true that at the relevant time, parties also

      agreed to appointment of a Receiver. That however will not preclude the

      parties to produce any documents relevant to the litigation as the

      settlement talks have failed.


20.   Even if it is considered that the documents were available to the party

      while filing their Defense, it cannot be said that the same cannot be

      produced now as the trial is yet to begin.

21.    In view of the above, I find no merits in the objection to the production of

       documents, and I permit the Applicant to produce the same by allowing

       the Application for Production dated 12.06.2026.


 (III) Application seeking leave to file Additional Reply filed by Respondent

       Nos.1, 2 and 3 dated 12.06.2026


22.    This Application for Leave has been necessitated in view of the Additional

       Documents that came to the notice / attention of respondent nos. 1, 2 and

       3. It is based on these documents that an Application for Amendment was

       filed. This Application seeking leave to file addition reply is a

       consequence of the Additional Documents and the proposed amendment.


23.    There is no dispute raised either by the Claimant or the Respondent No.4

       that the leave to file Additional Reply is being sought in view of the

       Additional Documents sought to be produced and the proposed

       amendment sought to be carried out. As by this common order, I have

       allowed the application for amendment as also the application for

       production of additional documents, as a consequence, this Application

       seeking leave to file Additional Reply is also being allowed. I therefore

       grant leave to the Respondent Nos. 1, 2 and 3 to file Additional Reply as

       annexed to the said Application and marked as Annexure A.
