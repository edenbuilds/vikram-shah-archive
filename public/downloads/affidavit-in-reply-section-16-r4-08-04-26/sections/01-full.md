1. I say that the Application filed by Respondent No. 2 under Section 16 of
        the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, (“Arbitration Act”)
        suffers from gross delay and laches and should be dismissed on that
        ground alone. Section 16(2) of the Arbitration Act, mandates that a
        plea that the arbitral tribunal does not have jurisdiction must be raised
        not later than the submission of the statement of defence. The extent of
        the delay is shown as below.

2. I say that the arbitrator was appointed by an Order of the Hon’ble High
        Court of Bombay at Goa dated 7th August 2024. Hereto annexed and
        marked ‘Annexure 1’ is a copy of the said Order.

3. Pursuant to the said Order, arbitration proceedings commenced on 26th
        August 2024 at which meeting the Ld. Arbitrator directed that Notice
        be issued inter alia to Respondent Nos. 1-3.

4. On 14 September 2024, Respondent Nos. 1-3 through their advocate
        appeared before the Ld. Arbitrator, proposed a global settlement and
        sought time to hold the settlement talks. Time was granted, and the
        arbitration proceedings were stood over to 25th January 2025. The
        Minutes of Proceedings which are hereto annexed and marked
        ‘Annexure 2’ also expressly recorded that all parties have unanimously
        decided that the seat and venue of the arbitration proceedings shall be
        Panaji, Goa.

5. On 25th January 2025, the Parties informed the Ld. Arbitrator that
        settlement talks had been unsuccessful. As recorded in the Minutes of
        Proceedings which are hereto annexed and marked ‘Annexure 3’, the
        Claimant filed his Statement of Claim dated 22nd January 2025, and the
        advocate on behalf of Respondent Nos. 1-3 stated that the opposing
        Respondents i.e.1-3 would file their Written Statement i.e. Statement of

8. Respondent Nos. 1-3 finally filed their Statement of Defence on 8th March

       Upon the filing of the Statement of Defence, the statutory period to

       stage which provides no justification for the delay is another tactic
       adopted by Respondent Nos. 1-3 to frustrate and delay the progress of
       the arbitration.

9. Further, after the filing of the Statement of Defence on 8th March 2025, the

18. With reference to paragraphs 6 to 14 of the Application, Respondent No.
       4 submits as under:

       (i) The contents of the said paragraphs pertain to the merits of the
              claim and defence, and they cannot form the subject matter of
              an Application under Section 16. Generally, applications under
              Section 16 deal with questions regarding the scope, effect and
              applicability of the arbitration clause, whether the arbitration
              clause is void or not and the validity of the constitution of the
              Arbitral Tribunal. In the present case the Tribunal was
              constituted by an Order dated 7th August 2024 passed by the
              High Court of Bombay at Goa under Section 11(6) of the
              Arbitration Act. The said Order for appointment of the Tribunal
              appears to have been passed by consent.

       (ii) The Association of Persons (“AOP”), operating under the name
              M/s PVM Associates, was formed for the specific purpose of
              developing properties in Bambolim, Goa, bearing Survey Nos.
              93/3 and 93/4. I say that Clause 35 of the said AOA explicitly
              mandates that "In the event of any dispute between the members
              of this association or between the members and the legal
              heirs... the matter shall be referred to arbitration".

       (iii) I say that the Respondents Nos. 1 to 3, as the legal heirs of Late
              Mr. Pascoal Trindade (who passed away on January 17, 2022),
              are legally bound by the AOA. Clause 28 of the AOA clearly
              states that "the legal heirs becoming members of the
              association will inherit all the rights and liabilities of the
              deceased member".

       (iv) I say that the Respondents have failed to fulfil their contractual
              obligations to defend the interests of the AOP in various courts,
              leading to the revocation of development permissions by the
              District Court on October 30, 2023, causing a loss of
              approximately Rs. 20 Crores.

(v) I say that the Respondents’ non-cooperation and their failure to
               implead themselves in Revision Applications (such as CRA No.
               41/2018) have led to the dismissal of vital legal protections for
               the properties. These defaults constitute a fundamental breach
               of the AOA, making the dispute squarely arbitrable under
               Clause 35.

       (vi)    The vesting of entitlements, rights and title of the subject
               properties into the Articles of Association dated 1st April 2013
               and whether the subject properties ceased to be the personal
               property of the deceased Pascoal Trindade pertain to the merits
               of the matter and cannot form the subject matter of an
               Application under Section 16 of the Arbitration Act.

       (vii)     I deny that the rights, entitlement, title, interest and/or
               possession of Respondent No. 2 in the said properties were
               never the subject matter of the Articles of Association as
               alleged. As recorded in the Joint Venture Agreement dated 25th
               April 2014, Respondent No. 2 had executed a Power of
               Attorney dated 9th December 1996 under registration no.
               8339/96 in favour of her husband the deceased Pascoal
               Trindade. The said Power of Attorney entitled the deceased
               Pascoal Trindade to act on her behalf. In the said Joint Venture
               Agreement, the deceased Pascoal Trindade, had stated that
               Respondent No. 2 and he jointly had a 37.85% share in the
               AOP namely M/s. PVM Associates. Hereto annexed and
               marked ‘Annexure 13’ is a copy of the said Joint Venture
               Agreement. It is hence denied that the Articles of Association
               are not binding or enforceable against Respondents 1, 2 and 3.

       (viii) It is further submitted that in any event, non-signatories may
               also be bound by an arbitration agreement. This legal principle
               has been laid down by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Chloro
               Controls India (P) Ltd. v Severn Trent Water Purification Inc.
               reported in (2013)1 SCC 641.

       (ix) The aforesaid principle has been further elaborated by the
               Supreme Court in Cox and Kings Ltd. v SAP India Pvt. Ltd &
               Anr. reported in (2025)1 SCC 611 and Ajay Madhusudan Patel
               v Jyotrindra S. Patel & Ors. reported in AIR 2024 SC 4648
               where the Supreme Court reiterated that non-signatories can be
               referred to arbitration where they are a veritable party to the
               arbitration agreement.

       (x) I deny that by a mere reading of the material relied upon by the
               Claimant demonstrates that the Articles of Association are not
               binding upon Respondent No. 2. The Articles of Association
               clearly state that that the deceased Pascoal Trindade brought
               and introduced into the Association properties then valued at
               Rs. 3,09,50,000 which is prima facie evidence that the
               properties were brought in. The defences raised by Respondent
               (which in any event are denied) would have to be ultimately
               proved by evidence and cannot be the subject matter of an
               Application under Section 16.
